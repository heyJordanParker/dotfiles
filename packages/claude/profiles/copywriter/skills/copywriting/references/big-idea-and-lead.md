# Big Idea and Lead

Two decisions sit between strategy and structure: the big idea (the one concept the whole piece hangs on) and the lead (the opening that earns the rest of the read). Get these right and the structure almost picks itself. Get them wrong and no framework saves the draft.

## The big idea

The big idea is one fresh, charged concept that makes the reader see their situation differently. It is not the product, not the offer, not the feature — it's the angle that reframes the reader's world so your solution becomes the obvious next thought.

Four tests; a real big idea passes all four.

- **Singular** — one idea, not three. If you can't say it in a sentence, it isn't an idea yet, it's a topic.
- **Surprising** — it shifts something the reader believed. A statement they'd nod at on reflex is not an idea, it's wallpaper.
- **Believable** — surprising but provable. An idea the reader can't accept is a stunt; back it with a mechanism or proof.
- **Relevant to their desire** — it bends toward the outcome the reader already wants. Clever that points away from their desire is decoration.

> Topic (not an idea): "Email automation for SaaS."
> Big idea: "Your churn isn't a product problem — it's a first-week problem, and the first week is the one thing you've automated worst."

The idea reframes (churn → onboarding), surprises (it's not the product), stays believable (most churn is early), and points at the desire (keep more customers).

## Angle generation

The same product holds many angles. The job is not to invent one — it's to find the several that exist and pick the one that matches the reader's awareness stage and the market's sophistication. Run the product through these sources and you'll have more angles than you can use:

- **The enemy** — name what's stealing the outcome (the manual process, the bloated competitor, the busywork). Readers rally against a villain faster than toward a benefit.
- **The mechanism / secret** — the specific *how* the reader hasn't heard. This is the level-3 sophistication move: a named mechanism restores belief a worn claim can't earn.
- **The contrarian take** — the thing everyone in the category says, inverted, with a reason. Strong against a tired market.
- **The timely trend** — a shift in the reader's world that makes acting now urgent and not-acting risky.
- **The identity** — who the reader becomes by using this. The level-5 move for cynical markets.
- **The cost of inaction** — what staying put quietly costs, made concrete and cumulative.
- **The dream outcome, made vivid** — the after-state rendered in sensory detail until the reader can feel it.

Generate several, then choose by match: which angle meets this reader where they already are (see awareness-and-sophistication.md)?

## Lead types

The lead is the first 10-20% of the piece — the part that decides whether the rest gets read. The lead type follows directly from the reader's awareness stage. Lead with what they're ready to hear.

- **Offer-led** — opens on the deal, the price, the terms. For the **most-aware** reader who already wants it. "Founding price ends Friday."
- **Promise-led** — opens on the bold result. For **solution-aware** readers weighing options. "Turn trials into customers without writing a single email."
- **Problem-led** — opens on the pain, then agitates. For **problem-aware** readers who feel it but haven't solved it. The SaaS workhorse. "You ship a feature. Nobody notices. Again."
- **Story-led** — opens on a character and a narrative. For **unaware** or skeptical readers who'd reject a direct pitch; the story slips past the guard. "Last March, a founder watched 40% of her signups vanish in seven days."
- **Secret-led (mechanism)** — opens on a surprising how, a hidden reason. For **solution-aware** readers in a tired market; the secret is the new mechanism. "The reason your onboarding emails fail has nothing to do with the copy."
- **Proclamation-led (big promise)** — opens on a sweeping, provable claim. For readers ready for a bold stance, backed immediately by proof so it doesn't read as hype.

## Structure serves the idea

The idea decides the lead; the lead decides the structure — never the reverse. Picking a framework first (see persuasion-structures.md) and pouring the idea into it produces copy that fits the template and misses the reader. Choose the structure that delivers *this* idea to *this* reader at *this* awareness stage. If the idea and the framework fight, the idea wins and the framework changes.
