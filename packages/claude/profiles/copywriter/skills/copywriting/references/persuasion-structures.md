# Persuasion Structures

A persuasion structure is the order an argument moves in to carry a reader from where they are to the action. These are channel-agnostic — the same skeleton drives a landing page, an email, or an ad; only the length changes. Pick the structure by the reader's awareness stage and the idea you're delivering (see big-idea-and-lead.md). The idea picks the structure, never the reverse.

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

Name the pain → make its cost felt → present the solution and the next step.

- **Fits:** problem-aware readers. The workhorse for SaaS, where most traffic feels a pain it hasn't solved.
- **Why it works:** recognition first. The reader who sees their problem stated precisely trusts that you understand the rest.

> Your projects slip and you find out at the retro. The status update goes out Friday, after the deadline already moved and the client already noticed. Flag a task the day it stalls, and the deadline gets saved while there's still room to move it.

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge

Current painful state → the desired future → your product as the bridge between them.

- **Fits:** problem-aware to solution-aware readers, transformation offers with a clear before and after.
- **Why it works:** it sells the gap. The vivid after-state creates the want; the bridge makes it reachable.

> Today, closing the month means a week of chasing receipts by hand. Imagine every expense already categorized and reconciled the moment it clears. That's what a bank-feed sync does — it reads each transaction, then files it.

## AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

Hook → build interest with relevance → turn interest to desire with proof and outcome → ask for the action.

- **Fits:** general funnels, solution-aware readers. A safe default when the reader is warm but not committed.
- **Why it works:** it escalates in the order attention actually decays — earn the next second before spending it.

## 4Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push

Promise the outcome → picture the reader living it → prove it's real → push to the action.

- **Fits:** solution-aware and product-aware readers, especially landing pages and long-form sales copy.
- **Why it works:** it pairs desire and belief in sequence — the picture builds the want, the proof immediately pays for it before doubt sets in, and the push arrives while belief is high.

> **Promise:** Roles that fill themselves while you sleep. **Picture:** You open your laptop to three qualified candidates already scheduled, none of whom you chased down. **Proof:** "We cut time-to-hire from six weeks to nine days." — named recruiter, with the dashboard. **Push:** Post your first role, see the first match today.

## PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story, Testimony, Offer, Response

Problem → amplify the cost → a story of someone who lived it → testimony and proof → the offer → the response you want.

- **Fits:** long-form, solution-aware to product-aware readers; sales pages and multi-email sequences where each element can be its own touch.
- **Why it works:** it earns a big ask by paying in full first — the story and testimony stack belief high enough to carry the offer.

## Star, Story, Solution

Introduce a character (the customer) → tell their challenge as narrative → reveal the result your product made possible.

- **Fits:** unaware and problem-aware readers who'd reject a direct pitch; case-study content and founder narratives.
- **Why it works:** a story slips past the reader's sales guard. They follow the character, then realize the result could be theirs.

## Feature-Benefit Bridge

Feature → "so that…" → "which means…". Walk each feature across to the outcome.

- **Fits:** product-aware readers, feature pages, comparison sections where the reader wants specifics.
- **Why it works:** it refuses to leave a feature stranded. Every capability arrives already translated into what it does for the reader.

> Auto-categorization (feature) → so each record files itself by rule instead of by hand (so that) → which means the right entry lands in the right place the moment it arrives (which means).

## Choosing among them

Map the structure to the stage:

- **Unaware** → Star-Story-Solution. Enter through narrative, not a pitch.
- **Problem-aware** → PAS or BAB. Lead with the pain they feel.
- **Solution-aware** → AIDA, 4Ps, or PASTOR. Lead with promise and mechanism.
- **Product-aware** → 4Ps or Feature-Benefit Bridge. Lead with proof and the edge.
- **Most-aware** → skip the long build; open on the offer and push (see pages.md for the offer-led upsell page).

When the idea doesn't fit any of these cleanly, build the structure the idea needs. The frameworks are starting shapes, not cages.
