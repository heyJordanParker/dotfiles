# Distribution Copy

Distribution copy goes out into a space you don't own — a feed, a search result, an inbox, a launch window — and pulls attention back. Unlike a page, you don't control the frame: the reader didn't come to you, the hook has to survive a feed it wasn't invited into, and you get one line before they scroll. Each asset has a recipe, a typical awareness stage, and a characteristic failure. Find the asset you're writing, build to its recipe, then run the draft back through the writing principles and the editing skill.

## Organic content

Organic content does **not** inherit conversion-page logic. There is no controlled frame, no nav stripped to a single forward path, no hard CTA — and a link in the post body is penalized or beside the point, so the link goes in a reply or a comment. The conversion is attention itself: a reply, a save, a comment, a ranking, not a click. The hook does the work a whole landing page does elsewhere, because it has to survive a feed the reader didn't open for you. These are written natively per platform — the mechanics below are unique to each surface and not interchangeable.

### X post

- **Job:** earn attention and replies with one idea native to the feed. No hard CTA — the conversion is the reply, the follow, the save.
- **Recipe:** a single post is a scroll-stopping first line that carries the whole idea, with no link in the body. A thread is a hook post → 5-7 standalone one-idea value posts → a close that asks for the reply, with any link in the first reply.
- **Stage:** unaware to problem-aware — enter on a sharp claim or a useful specific, never a pitch.
- **Moves:** the hook is ~80% of performance, so lead with the idea, not the brand (see headline-and-hook.md); links in the post body are penalized, so the link goes in a reply; replies and conversations are weighted far above likes, so ask a question; one idea per post.
- **Mistake:** burying the hook, a link in the body, or corporate brand-voice where the feed rewards a specific human point of view.

### LinkedIn post

- **Job:** build authority and reach among peers and buyers with a personal insight or story. The CTA is a comment, not a click.
- **Recipe:** a hook in the first ~210 characters (the "see more" fold) → a short story or framework in 1-2 sentence lines with whitespace → the takeaway → a soft CTA inviting a comment. The link goes in the comments; the sweet spot is ~1,300-1,900 characters.
- **Stage:** problem-aware to solution-aware professionals in a work feed.
- **Moves:** only ~210 characters show before the fold and most readers decide there, so the first line is the whole bet (see headline-and-hook.md); a personal-story hook outperforms a generic one severalfold; line breaks every 1-2 sentences for mobile; saves and comments are weighted above likes; substantive length beats very short.
- **Mistake:** an outbound link in the body, a weak first line, or a generic corporate take.

### Blog post

- **Job:** rank for a query and convert intent into a next step — and be the source AI answer engines cite.
- **Recipe:** title / H1 matching the query → the core question answered in the first 50-100 words (a quick-answer block) → an exhaustive body with subheads, examples, and data plus 2-3 internal links → one low-friction CTA.
- **Stage:** unaware / problem-aware for educational posts, product-aware for "best {category}" and "{competitor} alternative" pieces — match the query intent (see awareness-and-sophistication.md).
- **Moves:** match search intent exactly — one keyword, one intent per post; front-load the direct answer, which is what answer engines cite; cover the topic exhaustively and interlink it into a cluster; one CTA per piece, pointing at the page that continues the intent (see pages.md).
- **Mistake:** writing for the keyword instead of the reader, or ending with no single next step.

## Ads

Paid placement buys the impression; the copy has to earn the click and keep the promise. Whatever the ad says, the page it lands on must open on the same words — message match (see pages.md and awareness-and-sophistication.md). The two jobs a solo SaaS founder runs split on where the reader's head is when the ad reaches them.

### Search ad

- **Job:** earn the click from someone already searching, on the exact keyword, and set the expectation the landing page will continue.
- **Recipe:** headline on the searched query → one line of value or proof → CTA. Match the structure to awareness — a problem hook or PAS for the broad query, an offer for the high-intent one (see persuasion-structures.md).
- **Stage:** problem-aware to product-aware — they typed the query, so meet it on its own terms.
- **Moves:** the hook carries ~80% of performance — lead with the angle that matches the keyword, not the brand; whatever the ad promises, the landing page must open on the same words and continue the exact query (message match, see pages.md and awareness-and-sophistication.md).
- **Mistake:** an ad that wins the click on a promise the page doesn't keep — the worst money in marketing.

### Social ad

- **Job:** interrupt a feed and earn the click from someone who wasn't looking — the creative is the targeting.
- **Recipe:** native / user-generated-style creative → a thumb-stopping hook in the first line or first ~3 seconds → one line of value or proof → CTA. Lead cold readers with the problem, not the offer (PAS or a problem hook, see persuasion-structures.md).
- **Stage:** unaware to problem-aware — they didn't ask, so the creative has to create the want.
- **Moves:** the ad must not look like an ad — native, user-generated-style creative outperforms polished brand creative, which the feed trains readers to skip; the hook in the first line or first ~3 seconds carries ~80% of performance (see headline-and-hook.md); the page still must keep the promise (message match, see pages.md).
- **Mistake:** a creative that reads as an ad and gets skipped on sight — or a thumb-stopping click the page then fails to continue.

## Cold email

Cold email goes to one stranger who never opted in — off your own property, into an inbox you don't own, with no relationship to trade on. It is not lifecycle email: there's no prior trust, no behavior to trigger on, nothing earned yet. You earn the reply in a few lines by leading with the reader's world, not your pitch. The mechanical email craft is shared — the subject line as headline, the preheader, plain text over a template, body shape, the P.S., driving a reply — so build on the lifecycle reference rather than restating it (see email.md).

- **Job:** earn a reply from one stranger who never opted in — one human writing to one human, not a broadcast. The conversion is the reply, not a click.
- **Recipe:** a subject that reads like a person, not a campaign → a preheader that extends it, not echoes it → a first line about *them* (a specific, researched trigger) before a word about you → one sentence of relevance (why you, why now) → one clear ask → a short P.S. Keep it to a phone screen, plain text, one idea built to one ask (see email.md for the shared email craft — preheader, body shape, P.S.).
- **Stage:** unaware to problem-aware — they didn't ask, so open on their world, not your product (see awareness-and-sophistication.md).
- **Moves:** relevance is the whole game — a researched, specific first line ("saw you shipped X") beats any clever hook; lead with them and earn the pitch in one line; one ask only; short enough to read on a lock screen; the subject line is the headline and decides the open (see headline-and-hook.md); ask plainly for the reply (see email.md).
- **Mistake:** a templated blast that's obviously not written to this person — the mail-merge opener, the fake "just following up" on a thread that never existed, the manufactured familiarity. It reads as spam on sight and burns the address.

## Launch

A launch concentrates attention and a reason to act now into a short window. The page is yours to build; the channels you launch *on* are not — Product Hunt and Hacker News each have native rules, and the same announcement that wins on one gets flagged on the other. Across all of them the urgency has to be real: a fake deadline or manufactured scarcity, caught once, costs every future launch.

### Launch page

- **Job:** concentrate attention and a reason to act now into a short window.
- **Recipe:** Hero with the launch as news → demo or walkthrough → 3-5 highlights → before/after → early testimonials → launch pricing or early access → CTA with real urgency — built from the page section catalog (see pages.md).
- **Stage:** mixed, often your warmest audience first (list, community).
- **Moves:** the news *is* the hook; pair it with genuine why-now (founding price, early-access cap). Real urgency only — a fake deadline caught once costs every future launch.
- **Mistake:** manufactured scarcity. The internet is small; you will be caught.

### Product Hunt

- **Job:** win the day's attention on Product Hunt — the ranking, the comments, and the click through to your page. The maker's first comment does the persuading, not the tagline alone.
- **Recipe:** a tagline that says what it does and who it's for in one scannable line → a first comment / maker's note (why you built it, what it does, who it's for, one question back to the community) → fast replies to every comment, all day → a gallery that shows the product working, not a logo.
- **Stage:** problem-aware to product-aware — a curious, builder-heavy crowd that wants both the story and the substance.
- **Moves:** the tagline is the hook — clear over clever, the job it does not a slogan (see headline-and-hook.md); the maker's comment is where you actually sell — personal, specific, why-now; engagement drives the ranking, so reply to every comment through the day; rally your own audience early, but every vote has to be real.
- **Mistake:** a clever tagline nobody can parse, a corporate launch voice in a community that rewards a human maker, or gaming the votes — caught once, it sinks the launch and the reputation with it.

### Show HN / Hacker News

- **Job:** survive the front page and earn a technical, skeptical crowd — the title and the first comment carry the whole launch.
- **Recipe:** a "Show HN: {plain description}" title, literal and free of marketing adjectives → a first comment with what it is, why you built it, how it works, and an honest limitation → direct answers to every reply, including the critical ones. No hype, no growth-hacky framing.
- **Stage:** problem-aware, deeply technical, allergic to marketing language.
- **Moves:** the title must be plain and literal — Hacker News punishes adjectives and rewards "what it actually is" (see headline-and-hook.md); show the thing working with no signup wall in front of it; state a real limitation — admitting what it doesn't do buys more credibility than any claim; engage critics straight, since defensiveness reads worse than the flaw.
- **Mistake:** marketing voice on a channel that treats it as a lie — superlatives, a manufactured origin story, a signup wall, or a defensive reply to fair criticism.
