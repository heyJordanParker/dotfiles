# Lifecycle Email

One job per email, one CTA per email. Sequences compound; single sends nudge. Match the trigger to the moment, then run the draft back through the writing principles and the editing skill.

## Email types

- **Onboarding (new user)** — 5-7 emails over ~14 days, each driving one step toward the first win. Welcome + single next step → quick win → key feature → success story → check-in → advanced tip → upgrade prompt. Goal: activation. Mistake: dumping every feature in email one.
- **New customer** — 3-5 emails reinforcing the purchase and driving adoption (they've committed; reassure and expand, don't re-sell).
- **Step reminder** — one email or a short series when a high-value setup step stalls. Remind what they started, say why it matters, link straight to finishing it.
- **Trial-ending / upgrade to paid** — value summary (what they've accomplished) → what they're missing → social proof → the deadline → last chance. Trigger on behavior (hit a limit, used a premium feature) over time where you can.
- **Failed payment** — 3-4 emails, no guilt, assume an expired card. One CTA: update payment. Escalate clarity on what they'll lose, not the pressure.
- **Usage report** — recurring; show what they accomplished and the value delivered ("you saved 6 hours this month"). Make them feel the worth. Never send an empty report — it reminds them they don't use it.
- **Win-back (expired trial / churned)** — what's new since they left → the reason they left, addressed → an offer to return. No desperation; they return when their original reason is fixed.
- **Referral / expansion (happy, activated user)** — trigger on a success signal (a win hit, a milestone reached, a high-usage streak). One ask, never both at once: refer someone who'd get the same result, or expand — add seats, raise the plan, grow usage. Lead with the value they've already gotten and make the ask feel like more of it. Mistake: asking before the user has felt a win.

## Across all

- **The subject line is the headline** (see headline-and-hook.md) — it earns the open or the email doesn't exist. Front-load the ask; one reader, one action.
- **The preheader is the subject's partner** — the preview line beside the subject in the inbox, the second words the reader sees after the subject and the most-read text in the email after it. Don't waste it echoing the subject or leaking "view in browser" — extend the promise: the subject opens the loop, the preheader widens it. Roughly 40-90 characters. Mistake: leaving it blank so the inbox scrapes the first body line.
- **Body shape: one idea, built to one ask** — open with the reason the email exists, no warm-up. Keep it short and scannable — one thought per paragraph, the CTA on its own line — so a reader skimming only the bolded path still knows what to do. Every sentence earns the next; cut the one that doesn't. Mistake: burying the ask under three paragraphs of context.
- **The P.S. gets read** — many readers jump from the subject straight to the P.S., so it's the most-read line in the body after the opener. Spend it on the one thing that matters: restate the ask, name the deadline, or land the core benefit. It's a second shot at the CTA, never a sign-off afterthought.
- **Plain text beats a template** — a personal-feel, plain-text email lands better in the inbox and earns more replies than a designed template. Better inbox placement, a higher response rate.
- **Drive a reply on the early sends** — ask for a response on the first emails. A reply trains deliverability and opens a conversation.
