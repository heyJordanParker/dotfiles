# In-Product Microcopy

The reader is already inside the product, mid-task. Microcopy converts at the moment of value or friction — be brief, specific, and never trap, then run the draft back through the writing principles and the editing skill.

- **Signup** — fewest fields that let them start (email + password, or social auth). Show value before the form ("free, no credit card"). Labels stay visible, not just placeholders; errors are specific and keep the entered data. Mistake: asking for company, role, and phone before the reader has seen anything.
- **Paywall / upgrade** — fire after the aha moment, never before. Headline on the benefit behind the wall ("Get {benefit} — {feature} is on {plan}"), preview the value, mark the current plan, value-oriented CTA ("Start getting {benefit}"), and always a clear escape ("Not now"). Mistake: asking before value is felt, or hiding the close.
- **Popup** — value obvious enough to justify the interrupt; trigger on engagement (scroll depth, exit intent, a click), never on arrival. Headline = benefit or curiosity, one field, CTA in first person ("Get my guide"). The decline is polite, never a guilt-trip ("No thanks," not "No, I don't want to save money"). Mistake: a 5-second timer and a hidden X.
- **Empty state** — the reader's first screen with nothing in it. Make it the fastest path to the first win: one line on what goes here and why it's worth it, one action to create the first item. Treat it as onboarding, not décor. Mistake: an illustration and "Nothing here yet" with no next step.
- **Cancellation / churn-save** — the click on "cancel" is the moment to offer the lighter alternative, not a wall. Lead with the one that fits the reason for leaving: pause instead of cancel (keep the data, skip the bill), downgrade to a smaller plan, or fix the specific reason ("Too expensive?" → the cheaper tier; "Missing X?" → it's shipping / here's how). Keep the real exit one clear click away the whole time — never hide it, gate it behind extra steps, or guilt it ("Are you sure you want to lose everything?"). When they confirm, cancel cleanly and say what happens to their data and when access ends. Mistake: a multi-screen maze, a grayed-out cancel, or a "we'll miss you" with no actual alternative.
