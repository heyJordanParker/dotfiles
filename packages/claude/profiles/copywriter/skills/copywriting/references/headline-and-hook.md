# Headline and Hook

The headline does one job: earn the next line. Eighty percent of readers never get past it, so it carries more weight than the rest of the page combined. Write it to meet the reader at their awareness stage (see awareness-and-sophistication.md) — name the most advanced thing they already believe, then pull them one step on.

## Headline formulas by category

Formulas are starting shapes, not finished lines. Fill them with the reader's words and a specific outcome, then cut until only the claim is left.

### Outcome-focused

- **{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}** — "Understand how users experience your site without drowning in numbers."
- **{Achieve outcome} by {how the product makes it possible}** — "Generate more leads by seeing which companies visit your site."
- **Turn {input} into {outcome}** — "Turn one-time buyers into repeat customers."
- **{Achieve outcome} in {timeframe}** — "Get your tax refund in 10 days."

### Problem-focused

- **Never {unpleasant event} again** — "Never miss a renewal again."
- **{Question naming the main pain}** — "Hate chasing invoices?"
- **Stop {pain}. Start {pleasure}.** — "Stop chasing invoices. Start getting paid on time."

### Audience-focused

- **{Product type} for {audience}** — "Advanced analytics for Shopify stores."
- **{Product type} for {audience} to {use}** — "An online whiteboard for teams to brainstorm together."
- **You don't have to {skill} to {outcome}** — "You don't have to be an SEO pro to rank higher."

### Differentiation-focused

- **The {opposite of the usual way} to {outcome}** — "The no-spreadsheet way to forecast your cash flow."
- **The {category} that {differentiator}** — "The CRM that updates itself."

### Proof-focused

- **{Number} {people} use {product} to {outcome}** — "50,000 marketers use [product] to send better emails."
- **{Single sharp benefit}** — "Sound clear in every online meeting."

### Additional

- **The simple way to {outcome}** — "The simple way to track your time."
- **Finally, {category} that {benefit}** — "Finally, accounting software that doesn't fight you."
- **Get {benefit} from your {thing}** — "Get more revenue from the traffic you already have."
- **{Action} your {thing} like {admirable example}** — "Market your SaaS like a Fortune 500."
- **What if you could {outcome}?** — "What if you could close deals 30% faster?"
- **Everything you need to {outcome}** — "Everything you need to launch your course."
- **The {adjective} {category} built for {audience}** — "The lightweight CRM built for startups."

## Picking a formula by awareness stage

The formula category should match where the reader stands:

- **Unaware** → no product claim; open on their world or a startling fact (often a question or a proclamation).
- **Problem-aware** → problem-focused formulas. Name the pain they feel.
- **Solution-aware** → outcome-focused and differentiation-focused. Name the result and the path.
- **Product-aware** → differentiation-focused and proof-focused. Name the edge and the evidence.
- **Most-aware** → the offer is the headline. State the terms.

## The hook

The hook is the line immediately after the headline — the first line of body, the second line of an ad, the opening sentence of an email. The headline wins the glance; the hook wins the read. It pays off the headline's promise with one concrete, specific detail or one sharpening of the tension, and it never restates the headline. A hook that echoes the headline wastes the reader's most attentive second.

> Headline: "Your trials don't convert because your onboarding is one email."
> Weak hook (echo): "Onboarding is really important for trial conversion."
> Strong hook (sharpen): "The average SaaS sends a welcome email, then goes silent for 13 days — exactly the window where 60% of trials decide."

## The subheadline

The subheadline expands the headline by adding the specificity the headline had to leave out — the mechanism, the audience, or the proof. One or two sentences. If the headline is the promise, the subhead is the first reason to believe it.

> Headline: "Trials that convert themselves."
> Subhead: "Behavioral triggers send each user the exact nudge their actions call for — so you keep more customers without writing a single sequence."

## Section headlines

Body sections need headlines too, and the same rules apply: each section headline carries the section's one argument so a reader who only scans the headers still gets the whole case. A reader skimming your section headlines should be able to reconstruct the page's argument in order. Write them to be read alone.

## CTA copy

The button is a one-line headline for the action. Name what the reader gets, not what they do.

- **Weak:** Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Get Started.
- **Strong:** Start Free Trial, Get My Report, See Pricing for My Team, Create Your First Project.
- **Formula:** {action verb} + {what they get} + {qualifier if needed}. First person can lift it — "Start My Free Trial."
