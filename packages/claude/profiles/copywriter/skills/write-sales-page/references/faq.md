# FAQ

FAQs are objections in disguise: "Is this right for me?" means "I am not sure this is right for me." The FAQ sits immediately before the final CTA and closes with a contact line.

## Selection
- A question earns a slot only if a real prospect voiced the doubt (surveys, sales calls, support inboxes) — traced through Brief.md's objection list, not invented.
- The FAQ is the remainder set: doubts the body already handled do not repeat; the body answers each objection at the point it arises.
- A fake question is company-voiced ("What makes X so innovative?"), an SEO keyword in a question costume, or a question with no doubt behind it. Questions stay in the buyer's chair.
- 5-12 questions; median 6-7.

## Ordering
- First question: the fit/what-is doubt for cold traffic; the money doubt for priced self-serve; the category's one famous fear when it dominates.
- Group by doubt, frequency-ranked inside the group.
- Security and data doubts belong mid-to-late, never first.
- End on a fit-confirming or expansion question, so the last thing read is the reader recognizing themselves.

## Answer shape
- First clause kills the doubt outright ("Yes." / "No." / "Same day."), then mechanism, then proof — result-first.
- Doubt-shaped questions plus one-word-openable answers mean the collapsed question list alone reads as reassurance.
- Answer in the reader's register.

## Making the reader want more
- Convert the objection into proof ("clients ask who built the invoice tool because they want it for their own business").
- Reveal an unmentioned capability disguised as a question ("Can I use it with my team?") — the one place new surface area feels like answering, not pitching.
- Disclose one real limitation plainly; the honest "no" makes every "yes" credible. Never invent the limitation or the capability.
