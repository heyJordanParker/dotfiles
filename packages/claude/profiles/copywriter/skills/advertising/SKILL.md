# Advertising

What this advisor supplies: the paid-acquisition judgment the strategy call needs — which angles are worth testing, what awareness state the traffic arrives in, and an honest read on whether paid fits the product yet. The angle is the strategic unit; the headline is downstream of it.

## Does paid fit yet

Paid amplifies a working funnel; it doesn't create one. The honest gate for a solo founder:

- A landing page that already converts organic or referral traffic.
- Known unit economics — roughly what a customer is worth and what you can afford to acquire one for. Without this, paid spends money to learn nothing.
- A tracked conversion to optimize toward. Optimizing on clicks alone buys traffic, not customers.

If those aren't in place, the advice is usually "not yet — fix the funnel and learn the economics first," and you say it plainly. Launching paid before them pours budget through a leak.

## Match the angle to the awareness state

Cold traffic and search traffic arrive in different states, and the angle has to meet them where they are:

- **Paid social and display** — interruptive. The viewer wasn't looking for you; most are problem-unaware or only problem-aware. Lead with the problem or a pattern-interrupt, not the product name or a feature.
- **Search** — intent-driven. The searcher is solution- or product-aware and actively looking. Lead with the solution, the comparison, or the specific outcome.

The awareness model itself — the full stage ladder and what each stage demands of a headline, proof, and call-to-action — is the copywriter's to own. Here it's applied only to pick the entry angle for the channel.

## Angles

An angle is a distinct reason to click — a different motivation, not a reworded headline. Establish three to five distinct angles before any line gets written, so a test compares motivations instead of synonyms:

- **Pain** — "Stop losing hours to X."
- **Outcome** — "Get to Y in Z days."
- **Social proof** — "Join the 10,000 teams who…"
- **Curiosity** — "The X the best teams quietly rely on."
- **Comparison** — "Unlike X, we do Y."
- **Identity** — "Built for [the specific role]."
- **Contrarian** — "Why [the common practice] backfires."

Each angle should draw on a real pain, trigger, or outcome from research. An angle invented at the desk tests the founder's imagination, not the market.

## Paid big-picture

- **Platform fit** — search for high-intent capture, social for demand generation and visual products, LinkedIn for B2B where job-title targeting and a higher price point justify the cost.
- **Message match** — the ad and the page it lands on must make the same promise in the same words. A mismatch wastes the click, and it's the most common solo-founder paid leak.
- **Test angles before details.** Order of impact: angle and concept first, then hook, then visual, then body, then call-to-action. Testing button colors before angles spends effort at the wrong altitude.

## What good advertising judgment hands over

The angles worth testing, each tied to a research-backed motivation; the awareness and channel read for the traffic; and a straight answer on whether paid fits the product's stage yet.
