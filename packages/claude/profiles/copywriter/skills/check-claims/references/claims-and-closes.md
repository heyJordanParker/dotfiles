### Trace every fact to its source
Product, offer, founder, competitor — every claim traces to the facts file or the corpus. Offer facts (guarantee, trial, prices) appear verbatim or not at all. Unsourced escalates to the chief; it never auto-kills and never ships.
Never: invented founder workdays, fake customers ("Jane paid again in May"), competitor behavior absent from sources.

### Claim only events on the product's own timeline
The claimable timeline starts where the product acts. Events before the product is in play take attribution verbs only — tie and credit, never "run".
Never: "the product ran the launch" for a launch that happened before the product existed.

### Speak forward in closes
The close names a standing property of product-run pages; it never rescues the scene's own past artifact. Test: could the sentence be true for the exact ad or page the body just described? If not, reframe forward.

### State honest limits plainly, in the same breath as the claim they bound
The admission is proof, not fine print — the limit sits inside the row that makes the claim, not in a footnote.

### Show math, never write it
No arithmetic in prose, no paired-computation figures. One non-computational figure of the precedent class is legal ("a seven-figure funnel"). Worked examples are design elements.

### Build a page whose shape proves the claim
Structure is argument: a page that answers the buyer's questions plainly is itself the proof that the product knows. Scored at assembly, not per line.
