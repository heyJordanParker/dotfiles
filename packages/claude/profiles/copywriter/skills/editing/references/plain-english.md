# Plain English

Pompous word, plain twin. Line check L4 fails on any complex word here when its plain version carries the same meaning. Trimmed from the Plain English Campaign A-Z of Alternative Words (2001), the Australian Government Style Manual (2024), and plainlanguage.gov to the words that actually surface in SaaS copy — the legal and bureaucratic entries are dropped.

The test is not "is the long word wrong" — it's "does the plain word lose anything?" If it doesn't, the long word was costing the reader for nothing.

## Empty abstraction nouns

These nouns stand in for the concrete thing and drain the sentence — *case, character, nature, system, factor, feature*. There's no plain twin to swap in: cut the noun and name the thing it was hiding. Fail only the empty, padding use, never the word naming a real thing (an actual product feature, a real billing system).

- "acts of a hostile character" → "hostile acts"
- "his training was the great factor in his win" → "he won by being better trained"
- "questions of a personal nature" → "personal questions"

## Delete entirely

These add nothing. Cut them whole — no swap needed.

- a total of
- absolutely
- actually
- as a matter of fact
- at the end of the day
- at this moment in time
- basically
- currently (when *now* or nothing works)
- in due course (say *soon*, or say when)
- it should be noted that
- last but not least
- obviously
- of course
- quite
- really
- the fact of the matter is
- very
