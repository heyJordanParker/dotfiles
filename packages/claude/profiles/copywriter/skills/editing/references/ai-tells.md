# AI Tells & Slop

The anti-slop-editor's armory — every check in the Slop group runs against these. Anything an AI or a fill-in-the-blank template would produce, gone. Merged from the marketingskills AI-writing-detection list, the natural-transitions "transitions to avoid," and the offers banned-vocabulary, then trimmed to what shows up in SaaS conversion copy.

This list is scoped to **output copy**, not to internal docs. An em dash in a skill file is house style; an em dash crutch in a landing page is a tell.

## S1 — AI-tell phrases

The phrasings that mark text as machine-written. Fail on any.

**Openers**
- In today's fast-paced world / digital age / ever-evolving landscape
- In an era of…
- In the realm of… / in the landscape of…
- Imagine a world where…
- It's important to note that… / it's worth noting that…
- Let's delve into…

**Transitions**
- That being said… / with that in mind…
- At its core… / in essence… / fundamentally…
- To put it simply… / simply put…
- This begs the question…
- It's worth mentioning that…

**Closers**
- In conclusion… / to sum up… / all things considered…
- At the end of the day…
- In the final analysis…

**Overused verbs** (swap for the plain version)
- delve → explore, look at
- navigate → handle, work through
- foster → encourage, build
- unveil → show, launch
- underscore → stress, highlight
- bolster → strengthen
- empower → let, help

**Overused adjectives**
- robust → strong, reliable
- comprehensive → complete, full
- seamless → smooth, easy
- innovative → new
- cutting-edge → new, modern
- transformative → major
- crucial / pivotal / vital → key, important
- holistic → complete, whole

**Academic tells**
- shed light on → clarify, reveal
- pave the way for → enable, make possible
- a myriad of / a plethora of → many
- paramount → essential
- in terms of → for, about

## S2 — the em-dash rule

The em dash (—) is the single most reliable AI signature, because models reach for it as a sentence-variety shortcut. In output copy:

- At most **one** deliberate em dash per screen. A second fails.
- Most em dashes belong as a comma (parenthetical), a colon (introducing), or a full stop (two sentences).
- `The form—which is fast—captures leads` → `The form, which is fast, captures leads`.

## S3 — SaaS slop & empty superlatives

Words every competitor could paste onto their own page. They describe nothing because they describe everything.

- revolutionize, supercharge, unlock, elevate
- game-changing, next-level, world-class, best-in-class
- seamless / seamlessly, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art
- 10x, disruptive, paradigm shift, secret sauce

**Unbacked superlatives** — *the best way to…*, *the leading…*, *the #1…* with nothing behind the claim. Either back it (number, named customer, proof) or cut the superlative.

**Offer-language traps** (carry the same slop into pricing and launch copy):
- "Worth $X" / "$Y value" with no real comparable — inflation
- "Limited time" with no actual deadline — lying
- "100% guaranteed" with no stated conditions — risky
- "Secret / hidden / what they don't want you to know" — clickbait

The replacement for all of it is the same: a specific number, a named customer, a concrete outcome, a real timeline. Specificity beats the superlative every time.

## S4 — AI structural patterns

The giveaway shapes — a tell even when every word is clean. Fail on the structure.

- "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]…" — the rule-of-three list after *whether*
- "It's not just [X], it's [Y]…" — the additive not-X-but-Y inflation (not the contrarian "X isn't A, it's B" reframe, which is a real claim that corrects the reader)
- "By [verb-ing], you can [outcome]…" — a sentence opening on a gerund
- "Think of [X] as [elaborate metaphor]…"
- Three parallel clauses in a row, each the same length and shape

## Self-check

1. Read it aloud. Would a person say this to a colleague? If not, it's a tell.
2. Scan for clusters — one flagged word is a slip, three in a paragraph is machine-written.
3. Check em-dash count per screen.
4. Run the distinctiveness test (S5, in SKILL.md): cut each line and ask whether it would be missed. If not, the words were clean but empty.
