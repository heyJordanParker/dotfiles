# Write a sales Page

Use this after `copywriting.md` has shaped the shared claim-proof argument from `interview.md`. The failure it prevents: writing a feature brochure or fake-scarcity launch page instead of a Dent sales surface that sells one Offer and sends the reader to Checkout.

## Process

1. Confirm the sale and depth.

   - Consume `surface`, `page_job`, `audience`, `customer_situation`, `customer_language`, `promise`, `offer`, `differentiation`, `proof_points`, `proof_gaps`, `objections`, `reader_awareness_stage`, `market_sophistication`, `register`, `mood`, `visual_direction`, `constraints`, and `open_questions` from the brief.
   - Treat the typical reader as solution-aware or product-aware; scale longer for colder traffic or higher `market_sophistication`.
   - Sell one Offer and make the next action a Checkout path, a Link to Checkout, or the required purchase CTA from `constraints`.
   - Let the stage choose how much problem, mechanism, proof, and price framing the Page needs.

   IF `offer` does not name the paid Offer, price, terms, or included Products clearly enough to sell:
   ### Ask for the missing sale fact before writing
   The sales Page cannot invent the thing being sold.
   Never: fill an unknown guarantee, deadline, bonus, or price anchor from a common sales template.

2. Use the sales block order.

   - Hero: promise, stage-matched subheadline, proof hint, and primary CTA.
   - Picture the after: vivid before-to-after outcome in the reader's world.
   - Problem and agitation: name the pain, cost of inaction, and limiting belief.
   - Mechanism reveal: name why this Offer works when the alternative does not.
   - Solution intro: introduce the Offer as the mechanism made concrete.
   - Benefits: three to five outcome-first benefits, each with proof or a proof gap.
   - Proof: testimonials, demos, screenshots, numbers, founder credibility, case stories, or mechanism proof near decision points.
   - Offer stack: what is included, who it is for, terms, delivery, access, and Product value.
   - Price framing: honest anchor against a real alternative, cost of inaction, or payment term.
   - Risk reversal: guarantee, refund rule, trial, cancellation term, or support promise that actually exists.
   - FAQ and objections: answer late doubts before the final CTA.
   - Repeated CTA: repeat the Checkout action after fresh proof, after price framing, and at the close.

   ### Close the paid purchase argument
   A sales Page runs the full emotional and rational arc to Checkout.
   Never: stop after benefits with one lonely button and no price, proof, risk reversal, or objections.

3. Write the headline and mechanism from the reader's stage.

   - For problem-aware readers, lead with the costly problem and the promised shift.
   - For solution-aware readers, lead with the outcome and the differentiated mechanism.
   - For product-aware readers, lead with the Offer edge, proof, terms, or named alternative.
   - For most-aware readers, lead with the Offer and why acting now is honest.

   ### Make the mechanism specific when the market is tired
   A worn-out claim needs the believable reason it works.
   Example: "Turn every Article into a measured Funnel path without rebuilding your Site".
   Never: "Grow faster with the all-in-one platform".

4. Pair every major claim with proof.

   - Place `proof_points` beside the claim or CTA they support.
   - Mark or lower every `proof_gaps` claim before it reaches final copy.
   - Translate features into customer outcomes before naming Product mechanics.
   - Use comparison against `differentiation`, not against a strawman.

   IF a benefit cannot be proved:
   ### Write it as a plausible capability, not a result
   Capability copy can be true without pretending the outcome already happened.
   Never: turn "can track revenue by Link" into "doubles revenue from every Link".

5. Answer the objections that block Checkout.

   - Cover setup time, fit, price, commitment, refunds, migration, technical trust, delivery access, support, and whether this is different from the named alternative.
   - Put the answer where the doubt appears: proof near promise, price framing near price, risk reversal beside CTA, FAQ for late doubts.
   - Keep urgency real: current terms, dated price change, expiring cohort, limited capacity, or cost of waiting.

   ### Keep anchors and urgency honest
   One fake crossed-out price or fake deadline makes the true proof weaker.
   Never: invent bonus values, countdowns, scarcity, testimonials, or guarantees.

6. Write CTA copy that names the purchase motion.

   - Use the outcome or Checkout motion as the button text.
   - Repeat one primary CTA instead of adding alternate asks.
   - Attach risk reversal or expectation-setting microcopy to every major CTA.

   ### Send ready readers to Checkout
   The Page sells; Checkout completes.
   Example: "Start Checkout for the Course".
   Never: "Learn more" as the primary purchase CTA.

7. Route drafted copy through the final editing pass.

   AI tells, pompous wording, and wall-of-text cleanup before design → `writing/editing.md`
