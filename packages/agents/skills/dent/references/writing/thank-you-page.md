# Write a thank-you Page

Use this after `copywriting.md` has finished the shared copy argument from `interview.md`. The failure it prevents: ending a Funnel with a dead confirmation screen, vague gratitude, or a second hard sell before the Contact knows what happens next.

## Process

1. Confirm what just happened.

   - Consume `surface`, `page_job`, `audience`, `customer_situation`, `customer_language`, `promise`, `offer`, `differentiation`, `proof_points`, `proof_gaps`, `objections`, `reader_awareness_stage`, `market_sophistication`, `register`, `mood`, `visual_direction`, `constraints`, and `open_questions` from the brief.
   - Treat the reader as most-aware of the action they just completed.
   - Name the completed action: opt-in, Checkout, Order, Course access, Space access, booking, or Article next step.
   - Make the primary job reassurance and orientation, not persuasion.

   IF the preceding action is unclear from `page_job` or `offer`:
   ### Ask what the Contact just completed
   A thank-you Page cannot set expectations without the prior action.
   Never: write "You're all set" when the next delivery, access, or fulfillment path is unknown.

2. Use the thank-you block order.

   - Confirmation hero: clear success headline and one sentence that names what happened.
   - Expectation setting: delivery timing, inbox instruction, access path, receipt, or next communication.
   - One next step: click, check email, open the Course, join the Space, schedule, share, or continue reading.
   - Support and trust: what to do if the Contact does not receive access or has a problem.
   - Optional bridge: one contextual next Offer, Article, or Funnel Step only when it helps the completed action.

   ### Give one primary next step
   The Contact should know exactly what to do now.
   Never: show a menu of unrelated Offers, social links, and generic resources before the promised next step.

3. Write the headline as confirmation.

   - State the completed action in plain language.
   - Use the same promise and labels the previous Page or Funnel Step used.
   - Keep the tone aligned with `mood` and `register`, but make clarity win over cleverness.

   ### Reduce post-action anxiety first
   Confirmation copy earns trust by answering "Did it work?" immediately.
   Example: "You're in — check your inbox for the checklist".
   Never: "The journey begins" when the Contact needs delivery instructions.

4. Answer fulfillment objections.

   - Cover when access arrives, where to find it, what email address it came from, what happens to the Order, and how to get help.
   - Use `constraints` for required receipts, legal text, Course links, Space links, or Support paths.
   - Keep `proof_points` small and reassuring: real customer note, guarantee reminder, or delivery credibility.

   IF a bridge Offer appears:
   ### Make the bridge secondary to fulfillment
   The next Offer must feel like help after success, not a bait-and-switch.
   Never: hide the download, receipt, or access instructions beneath a new sales pitch.

5. Write CTA and support microcopy.

   - Make the primary CTA the immediate next step.
   - Use a secondary support line for delivery problems.
   - Keep decline or skip paths polite when there is an optional bridge.

   ### Preserve the good feeling
   A thank-you Page should make the Contact glad they acted.
   Example: "Open the Course" or "Check your inbox".
   Never: "Buy now before this disappears" unless the brief proves a real post-purchase Offer and timing reason.

6. Route drafted copy through the final editing pass.

   AI tells, pompous wording, and wall-of-text cleanup before design → `writing/editing.md`
