# Edit the copy

Use this after `copywriting.md` and the page-type writing leaf have produced drafted copy. The failure it prevents: sending AI-tell copy, vague SaaS claims, pompous wording, or wall-of-text blocks into the design step.

## Process

1. Consume the draft as the copy to ship.

   - Keep the Page, Funnel Step, or Article job from the brief.
   - Keep one Offer, one primary CTA, and every marked `proof_gaps` note.
   - Preserve Dent terms exactly: Page, Funnel Step, Offer, Checkout, Order, Contact, Course, Space, Article, Link, Designer.
   - Return edited copy, not an audit report, unless a claim cannot be cleaned without missing proof.

   IF a line depends on invented proof, false urgency, fake scarcity, fake guarantee, or unsupported results:
   ### Lower the claim or mark the proof gap
   Rewrite around what the brief proves, or leave an inline proof gap for the operator.
   Never: clean up a false claim so it sounds more believable.

2. Strip AI tells and lazy copy patterns.

   - Cut AI openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In today's digital age", "In an era of", "In the realm of", "In the landscape of", "Imagine a world where", "It's important to note that", "It's worth noting that", "Let's delve into".
   - Cut AI transitions: "That being said", "With that in mind", "At its core", "In essence", "Fundamentally", "To put it simply", "Simply put", "This begs the question", "It's worth mentioning that".
   - Cut AI closers: "In conclusion", "To sum up", "All things considered", "At the end of the day", "In the final analysis".
   - Replace AI verbs with plain verbs: delve → look at; navigate → handle; foster → build; unveil → show; underscore → stress; bolster → strengthen; empower → let or help.
   - Replace AI adjectives with plain adjectives: robust → strong or reliable; comprehensive → complete; seamless → smooth or easy; innovative → new; cutting-edge → modern; transformative → major; crucial, pivotal, vital → key or important; holistic → complete.
   - Replace academic tells: shed light on → clarify; pave the way for → make possible; a myriad of or a plethora of → many; paramount → essential; in terms of → for or about.

   ### Remove the em-dash crutch
   Output copy gets at most one deliberate em dash per screen. Change the rest to commas, colons, full stops, or tighter sentences.
   Example: `The form—which is fast—captures leads` → `The form captures leads fast.`

   ### Cut SaaS slop and empty superlatives
   Remove revolutionize, supercharge, unlock, elevate, game-changing, next-level, world-class, best-in-class, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, 10x, disruptive, paradigm shift, and secret sauce unless the brief proves the exact claim.
   Never: "the best way to", "the leading", or "the #1" without a number, named customer, result, or other real proof beside it.

   ### Remove fake Offer language
   Cut "Worth $X" or "$Y value" without a real comparable, "Limited time" without an actual deadline, "100% guaranteed" without stated terms, and "secret", "hidden", or "what they don't want you to know" clickbait.

   ### Break AI structures
   Rewrite "Whether you're X, Y, or Z", "It's not just X, it's Y", "By doing X, you can Y", "Think of X as [elaborate metaphor]", and three same-shaped clauses in a row.
   Keep a real contrarian claim only when it corrects the reader: `X is not A; it is B.`

   ### Keep distinctiveness after cleanup
   If a competitor could paste the line onto their own Page unchanged, rewrite it around the specific Contact, proof, mechanism, Link source, Funnel Step, or Offer.

3. Put the draft in plain English.

   - Swap pompous words for plain words when the plain word keeps the meaning.
   - Cut empty abstraction nouns when they hide the concrete thing: case, character, nature, system, factor, feature.
   - Cut filler entirely when it adds nothing.

   ### Delete empty filler
   Cut a total of, absolutely, actually, as a matter of fact, at this moment in time, basically, currently when `now` or nothing works, in due course, it should be noted that, last but not least, obviously, of course, quite, really, the fact of the matter is, and very.

4. Clean every line for readability.

   - Make each sentence clear on one read.
   - Use active verbs: `the form captures leads`, not `leads are captured by the form`.
   - Cut words the Contact does not need.
   - Fuse thin sentence chains when one clean sentence carries the idea.
   - Front-load the key point unless one deliberate periodic sentence earns the delay.
   - Use positive form: say what the thing is, not what it is not.
   - Keep related words together so modifiers point at the right thing.
   - Vary sentence length so the copy does not thud in the same rhythm.

   ### One line carries one idea
   Split any sentence, bullet, or paragraph that asks the reader to track two arguments at once.

5. Format the copy for scanning before design.

   - Keep paragraphs short: one to two sentences for Page and Funnel Step body copy; three only when the thought cannot split cleanly.
   - Put one idea per bullet.
   - Start bullets with the useful outcome, then add proof, condition, or detail.
   - Make section headings and subheads carry meaning; a scanner should understand the argument from headings alone.
   - Front-load sentences with the Contact's outcome, risk, proof, or next action.
   - Use concrete nouns and verbs before abstract claims.
   - Bold only load-bearing phrases the scanner needs: the promise, proof, condition, price term, risk reversal, or action.
   - Remove decorative bolding, bolded whole paragraphs, and emphasis that only makes a weak line louder.
   - Add whitespace between sections, proof blocks, forms, FAQ items, and CTA groups.
   - Break wall-of-text blocks into heading, body, bullets, proof, CTA, or microcopy.

   ### Headings must say something
   Replace vague headings such as "Overview", "Benefits", "Features", "Introduction", and "Conclusion" with the claim or useful promise the section proves.

6. Return the clean copy for design.

   - Output the edited copy in the same section order the writing leaf used.
   - Include headline, subheadline, CTA, body copy, bullets, proof, FAQ, form labels, and microcopy when they exist.
   - Keep inline proof gaps where proof is missing.
   - Do not author Designer JSON.
   - Do not add layout instructions beyond copy grouping, headings, bullets, bold phrases, and whitespace breaks.
